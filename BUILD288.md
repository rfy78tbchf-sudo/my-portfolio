# Build288 — commit history, current evidence and settlement dates together

The history writer clears a derived settlement date when the incoming ledger does not supply one. The worker restored it in a later RPC, after saving and retiring settlement evidence. An interruption between calls could leave a committed missing date even when evidence was available.

A service-only history bundle now performs the existing ledger write, evidence chunks, reactivation, revision retirement, date linking and evidence completion marker in one database transaction. Linking runs after the incoming evidence set is active; an older revision cannot restore a date before a correction is examined. Any stage failure rolls back the ledger, evidence, derived dates, success log and completion marker together. The worker sends the same normalized data through one RPC. Matching rules, known-date conflict rules and financial normalization do not change. Missing or mismatched evidence remains unverified.

The migration only creates the bundle function, checking the expected writer and linker definitions first. It does not invoke collection or modify stored financial rows, finalized monthly records, source observations or the initial baseline. The existing writer and linker definitions stay unchanged. The new function has an empty search path, requires the service role and grants no public/client execution. No schedule or workflow is added; existing settlement and partial-page CI tests cover the new path.

Synthetic integration tests reproduce the old committed date gap and check literal order/settlement dates and cash on repeated resync. Corrected evidence with a conflicting known order must leave settlement unverified. Injected failures in evidence save, revision retirement, linking, completion marking and the second evidence chunk must roll back every write. Oversized/malformed bundles and client roles are rejected. This closes an interruption window; it does not eliminate all repeated writes or complete whole-account A/B reconciliation.

Pages remains Build282. Worker deployment, exact operating source/privilege checks and the next normal history collection must be distinguished from synthetic tests. No physical iPhone verification is claimed.
